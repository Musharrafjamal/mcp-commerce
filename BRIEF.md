# Take-Home Assignment — AI-First Commerce Operations Challenge

### The challenge

An online commerce business frequently depends on engineers to investigate and resolve operational problems involving areas such as orders, payments, inventory, and fulfillment.

Design and build an AI-native solution that makes the operations team more independent.

The solution must include a remotely hosted MCP server written in TypeScript. The MCP should be a central part of the product, not a decorative or checkbox integration.

You are responsible for determining:

The user and problem you will focus on

The product workflow

The data and systems involved

The MCP capabilities

The appropriate technical architecture

The safety and operational considerations

What is in and out of scope

Treat us as the client. Ask questions, communicate assumptions, propose your approach, and provide progress updates.

We expect extensive use of AI coding tools, but you remain responsible for every decision and submitted result.

### Time and scope

You will have one week to complete the assignment. We expect approximately three to four hours of focused work.

Please bound the scope accordingly. We do not expect a complete product, and thoughtful prioritization is part of the evaluation. A small, coherent solution is preferable to a broad or polished implementation. Incomplete work is acceptable when you clearly explain the tradeoffs, limitations, and next steps.

### Requirements

Use TypeScript.

Build and host a remotely accessible MCP server.

Use only synthetic, public, or self-created data. Do not use real customer data or production credentials.

Demonstrate at least one coherent, end-to-end commerce-operations workflow.

Use Codex, Claude Code, or comparable AI coding tools extensively.

Include focused verification of the behavior that matters to your chosen workflow.

### What not to overbuild

You are not expected to build:

A frontend or design system

Authentication or user-management infrastructure

A complete commerce backend

Complex deployment or CI/CD infrastructure

Use existing libraries, templates, mocks, and AI-generated scaffolding wherever appropriate. Spend your time on product decisions, MCP design, backend correctness, and getting the core workflow working in a hosted environment.

We are not looking for the largest submission or the most features.

### How we expect you to communicate

This assignment is intentionally open-ended so that we can see how you collaborate, reduce ambiguity, and bound the scope with us.

Do not work in isolation and return only with a finished product. Keep us involved throughout the process by sharing questions, assumptions, proposed scope, product and technical decisions, progress, blockers, changes in direction, and tradeoffs as they arise.

Proactive and frequent communication is strongly encouraged. We are evaluating how you work with us as much as the final result, so involve us whenever our context or feedback could affect a decision.

All assignment-related communication—including questions, scope approval, progress updates, blockers, and submission—should remain in the existing email thread. Applications and formal hiring decisions will also remain on email. Once hired and offered the engagement, you will be added to the team's Slack workspace for ongoing work.

### What to submit

We will primarily review your asynchronous demo and deployed solution. Please make it possible for us to understand and test the core workflow without setting up the project locally.

Hosted MCP URL

Source repository

Concise README with setup and usage instructions

Product decisions, assumptions, and exclusions

Focused tests or equivalent runtime verification for important behavior

A four-to-five-minute asynchronous video recorded with Loom, OBS, or a similar tool. Demonstrate the deployed solution end to end, including the main workflow, how the MCP is used, and the most important product or technical decisions.

An AI worklog covering:

The AI tools and specific models you used

Why you selected particular models for planning, research, implementation, debugging, testing, or review

How you used AI to plan and break down the work

How you divided responsibilities between yourself and AI

Important prompts, instructions, or context you supplied

At least one AI suggestion you corrected, rejected, or substantially changed

How you verified AI-generated work

Remaining risks or unfinished work

A complete private transcript of your AI conversations is not required.

### Evaluation emphasis

We will primarily evaluate product judgment and technical competence, with particular emphasis on the MCP.

The MCP should be central to the solution rather than a thin wrapper added only to meet the requirement. We will look at how well its capabilities, inputs, outputs, descriptions, deployment, and safety decisions support the product workflow.

Communication, collaboration, and responsible AI usage will be considered throughout. Feature count, frontend polish, deployment-provider choice, and volume of generated code are not evaluation criteria.

Strong submissions will demonstrate:

Useful questions, explicit assumptions, and active collaboration

A well-bounded problem and coherent user workflow

A thoughtfully designed, remotely hosted MCP that an AI consumer can use effectively

Clear product and technical tradeoffs

Sound TypeScript and backend engineering

Appropriate judgment around failure modes and safety

Evidence that important behavior was actually verified

Deliberate model selection and responsible supervision of work done by LLM’s
